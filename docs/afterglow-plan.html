00 · THE ANSWERSelected concept, strongest evidence, biggest uncertainty

Selected concept

A fleet-aware, observation-aware burning calendar

For any area, Afterglow shows when burning was normal, unusual or critical across 2003 to now. It puts every satellite on one reference scale through calibrations learned where the sensors overlapped. It separates "no fire" from "not observed", and every verdict carries an uncertainty range.

Signature moment: retire Terra, Aqua and Suomi NPP with one click, as will happen by late 2027. The raw calendar breaks. Afterglow's verdicts mostly hold, and where they don't, it says so.

Strongest supporting evidence

FactNASA plans to end Terra MODIS data collection in January 2027 and Aqua MODIS around September 2027. CAMS began adding VIIRS to its fire system in December 2025 for this reason.

FactSuomi NPP product delivery is scheduled to stop on 1 November 2026 (date subject to change).

FactThe MODIS fire user guide warns that fire-pixel files carry no cloud or missing-data information. It also warns that time series binned at under about eight days can be severely biased.

Biggest uncertainty

FactThe official FAQ says teams "are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon." Doing 80% of the work early is therefore not allowed. The goal changes to 80% of research, decisions and rehearsal before 13 November, and none of the product.

HypothesisScientific risk: the VIIRS 750 m daily cloud mask may not be a good enough stand-in for when the 375 m product could see the ground.

What Afterglow adds beyond the obvious entry. The obvious entry merges hotspots on a map, adds a calendar heatmap and z-scores the counts. Afterglow adds four things that entry cannot provide.

  1. A "not observed" state per calendar cell, computed from NASA's daily fire masks.
  2. Anomalies that stay comparable across sensor changes, tested on held-out years.
  3. Uncertainty ranges that widen when coverage is poor or the satellite fleet is thin.
  4. Static industrial heat sources (likely including brick kilns) separated from vegetation burning.

Branding, extra panels or a chatbot would not add anything comparable.

03 · RESEARCHFindings that change the design

Each finding follows the chain evidence → why it matters → what it changes in our design. Labels show how solid each piece is.

A. The satellite fleet is changing during the hackathon

Three of five record-keeping satellites stop within about ten months

EvidenceFactTerra MODIS data collection ends January 2027 and Aqua around September 2027. Both are drifting out of their designed orbits. Suomi NPP delivery stops 1 November 2026. CAMS added VIIRS to its fire system on 4 December 2025 to prepare. MattersFrom 2027, every "is this year unusual?" judgement compares NOAA-20/21 VIIRS observations against a baseline that MODIS and S-NPP mostly built. Done naively, it compares two different instruments. ChangesRecommendationMake fleet transition the central problem and the demo hook. Build calibration "bridges" through the overlap years: NOAA-20 → S-NPP (2020–2026), then S-NPP → Aqua (2012–2026).

Operational systems already rely on a single "reference satellite", and Aqua is that satellite

EvidenceFactA July 2026 panel from INPE's Programa Queimadas maps accumulated fire foci from its reference satellite "AQUA_M-T", visible in a search snippet. I could not open the PDF, so INPE's transition plan is unknown. MattersAnalysts keep series comparable by sticking to one sensor. When Aqua ends, that practice breaks unless someone provides a calibrated successor. ChangesUse Aqua MODIS as Afterglow's reference scale: units are "Aqua-MODIS-equivalent detections". This matches the analyst practice and Aqua shares VIIRS's early-afternoon overpass.

B. "No detection" is not "no fire"

Hotspot files carry no information about clouds or missing data

EvidenceFactThe MODIS C6 user guide (§7.1.1) warns that fire-pixel location files contain no cloud or missing-data information, which can produce misleading or even incorrect results. The FIRMS FAQ points users to Level-3 or CMG products when cloud and missing data matter. MattersIn monsoon Bangladesh, an empty July may be cloud, not a lack of fire. A raw calendar would call it "below normal". ChangesCompute clear-view coverage from the daily L3 fire masks (MOD14A1, MYD14A1, VNP14A1, VJ114A1, VJ214A1). Their classes include cloud (4), non-fire land (5) and fire (7–9). Show a hatched "No clear view" state instead of a colour.

Daily MODIS calendars are biased by orbit sampling

EvidenceFactThe same guide warns of severe temporal and spatial biases in MODIS fire time series using intervals shorter than about eight days. MattersA day-by-day "burning calendar" invites wrong conclusions. ChangesThe anomaly calendar uses 8-day periods aligned to the MODIS L3 compositing (46 per year). Daily raw detections stay available in the period inspector as evidence, not as verdicts.

NASA already has a cloud-corrected MODIS fire count we can check against

EvidenceFactThe MODIS CMG products (MOD14CMQ/MYD14CMQ, 0.25°, monthly and 8-day) include a "CloudCorrFirePix" layer. Giglio, Csiszar & Justice (2006) used corrected counts to map fire seasonality: peak month, season length and periodicity. MattersOur coverage correction is not new for MODIS. What is new is applying it consistently across VIIRS and MODIS and exposing it per period in the UI. ChangesUse a 0.25° grid to match CMG, validate our MODIS coverage correction against CloudCorrFirePix, and cite this as the method's foundation. If our own L3 ingest fails, CMG is the fallback for MODIS.

C. The MODIS–VIIRS gap is large and not constant

Published ratios vary by region and land type

EvidenceResearch claimThe published ratios differ by study and region:
  • Punjab croplands: VIIRS detected 4.8× more fires than Terra+Aqua and 6.5× more than Aqua (Vadrevu & Lasko 2018).
  • South/Southeast Asia: about 7× and 5× (Vadrevu et al. 2019).
  • Northeast Asia, matched within 5 minutes: MODIS missed 65% of VIIRS fire pixels in forests and 83% in low-biomass land (Fu et al. 2020).
  • Africa: MODIS underestimated daily FRP by at least 42.8%, varying with view angle and grid size (Li et al. 2020).
 MattersOne global "multiply MODIS by 4" factor is wrong, and wrong in ways that depend on the place, the land type and the sensor geometry. ChangesCalibrate bridges per stratum (region cluster × land-cover class × day/night). Test a single constant ratio explicitly as a baseline that should lose.

VIIRS has a MODIS-like product and a high-resolution one

EvidenceFactThe VIIRS C2 user guide (v1.2, June 2025) makes four relevant points:
  • VNP14 (750 m) adapts the MODIS C6 algorithm and performs "more comparable to" Aqua MODIS.
  • VNP14IMG (375 m, the one FIRMS distributes) found about 3× more daytime and about 17× more night-time fire pixels than VNP14 globally in 2021.
  • A Lower Mekong example on 1 Mar 2016: Terra 606, S-NPP 9,643, Aqua 361 daytime fire pixels.
  • The 375 m swath fire mask has 10 classes, including cloud, bow-tie deletion and glint.
 MattersNight-time detections differ the most. Day and night must be modelled separately. ChangesBridges are fitted separately for day and night. The 750 m L3 masks provide the coverage denominator for VIIRS, explicitly labelled as a proxy for 375 m observability.

Duplicate detections and view angle distort counts

EvidenceFactVIIRS residual bow-tie fire pixels are flagged in the L2 algorithm QA (bit 22) so FRP is not double-counted. MODIS pixels grow strongly toward the swath edge, while VIIRS limits pixel growth (FIRMS FAQ). Research claimFu et al. (2020) and Zheng et al. (2026) both remove duplicate detections before comparing sensors. MattersRaw pixel counts inflate with geometry, not burning. ChangesDeduplicate VIIRS pixels within the same overpass. Use scan/track (pixel size) as a covariate. Report "fire-pixel detections", never "fires".

D. Not every hotspot is a vegetation fire

Static industrial sources contaminate calendars

EvidenceFactScience-quality monthly files carry a type field: 0 vegetation, 1 volcano, 2 other static land source, 3 offshore. A bug mislabelled type-2 sources as type-0 in VIIRS versions 1–2; it was fixed in version 3 (May 2025). The MODIS guide notes that static sources are incompletely flagged. FIRMS publishes a Static Thermal Anomalies mask built on a 400 m grid from 2023 S-NPP data. NRT data has no type attribution. MattersHypothesisBangladesh brick kilns operate through the dry season (roughly Nov–Apr), the same months as vegetation burning. They may appear as persistent hotspots and inflate "burning" in central districts. ChangesUse the type field from SP data where present, plus our own 400 m persistence filter, which a grid hash computes in O(n). Show excluded counts in the inspector with a toggle to include them. Verify a sample against imagery.

E. Users and workflows

Who decides what

EvidenceFactThe challenge names early-warning and emergency responders, scientists and land managers. GFW (now Global Nature Watch) offers weekly fire alerts compared with the historical average and "significant fires" views (blog, 2020). CONAE runs a hotspot catalogue and the GeoAvisos email alerts for MODIS and NPP hotspots. Research claimIn the Chittagong Hill Tracts, 88% of wildland fires are attributed to shifting (jhum) cultivation (Farukh et al. 2023). Jhum slash-and-burn runs January to May (ICIMOD record). MattersHypothesisA district-level analyst's real question is: "Is this season's burning unusual enough to escalate, and can I defend that conclusion?" The defence is what current tools don't support across sensor changes. ChangesDesign the main view around one verdict sentence with evidence. Before 28 Oct, interview at least three analysts or experts (for example forestry, geography or disaster-management faculty, or contacts from the Local Lead). Treat every user need as a hypothesis until then.

Baseline we must beat, and what Afterglow adds

Baseline (we build it too, as the control): a hotspot map plus a years × periods calendar of raw detections (MODIS + VIIRS merged, or VIIRS only), with a z-score against the historical mean.

  1. Coverage: every cell says whether the ground was actually seen. Low coverage shows as "No clear view", not as "low fire".
  2. Continuity: verdicts are on one reference scale, so 2026 (NOAA-20) can be judged against 2003–2022 (mostly MODIS). This is tested on held-out years.
  3. Honesty: 80% and 95% ranges that widen with poor coverage, thin bridges or a reduced fleet. Verdicts use the probability of exceeding the baseline, not a point value.
  4. Cleaner signal: static industrial sources are separated from vegetation burning, with counts shown.

Central contribution as a testable claim

Claim H1. We will test this on held-out years 2023–2026 in at least two contrasting regions (Bangladesh, plus Punjab or the Paraná Delta). We compute 8-day anomaly classes (below / typical / above) from NOAA-20 data alone through Afterglow's bridges. We expect these to agree with classes computed from Aqua MODIS against its own 2003–2022 baseline better than four baselines:

  • (a) raw NOAA-20 counts against the MODIS baseline;
  • (b) a single constant VIIRS/MODIS ratio;
  • (c) per-region-month ratios without coverage normalization;
  • (d) a short NOAA-20-only baseline (2020–2022).

We also expect Afterglow's 80% ranges to contain the Aqua-observed value in 70–90% of held-out cell-periods.

How we evaluate it: Cohen's κ for class agreement, with 95% confidence intervals from a year-block bootstrap, plus empirical coverage of the ranges.

What would disprove it: if κ(Afterglow) − κ(b) has a confidence interval that includes zero in both regions, the bridge model adds nothing over a constant ratio. If interval coverage falls below 60% or above 97%, the ranges are miscalibrated. If coverage normalization does not reduce the correlation between anomalies and clear-view fraction, the "not observed" contribution is cosmetic. Each outcome would be reported, not hidden.

Caveat: the Aqua-observed class is the target of a continuity claim. It is not ground truth about fire.

06 · SCIENCEA defensible method

What the prototype addresses, and what stays unresolved

Addressed

  • Detection sensitivity and resolution, through bridges stratified by land cover and day/night (and FRP in the advanced method)
  • Observation coverage, through clear-view land km²-days from L3 masks
  • Overpass-time differences: Terra morning vs afternoon sensors via separate bridges; NOAA-20's roughly 50 minutes earlier crossing via the NOAA-20 → S-NPP bridge
  • Repeated detections, through VIIRS same-overpass deduplication
  • Static sources, through the type field plus a persistence filter
  • Processing versions: SP science archive only for the baseline (MODIS C6.1, VIIRS C2 v3); NRT labelled provisional

Unresolved (shown as limitations)

  • The 375 m observability proxy: VIIRS coverage comes from the 750 m L3 mask
  • L3 daily masks are maximum composites, so the number of looks per day is not counted
  • Fires under cloud are assumed to burn at the clear-sky rate; rain suppresses fire, so this can overestimate
  • Aqua's own orbit drift in later years (a reference-scale threat)
  • View-angle effects only approximated; full scan geometry needs L2 data
  • No independent ground truth for Bangladesh

Method

s ∈ {Terra, Aqua, S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21} c = 0.25° cell p = 8-day period (46/yr) y = year D[s,c,p] vegetation fire-pixel detections (type 0, confidence ≥ nominal, deduplicated, static-masked) O[s,c,p] clear-view land km²-days = Σ_days Σ_1km-land-cells 1[FireMask ∈ {5,7,8,9}] from the sensor's L3 mask f[s,c,p] coverage = O / (land km² × days) ρ[s,c,p] observation-aware rate = D / O (detections per 1,000 clear-view km²-days) Bridge (simple method, must-have), fitted only on training years where both sensors observe: D_ref ~ NegBin(μ, φ), log μ = log O_ref + α[k,dn] + β[k,dn] · log(ρ_s + ε) + season[k] k = stratum (region cluster × land-cover class), dn = day/night chain: NOAA-21 → NOAA-20 → S-NPP → Aqua (reference); Terra → Aqua directly Harmonized index H[c,p,y] = inverse-variance combination (log scale) of each available sensor's Aqua-equivalent estimate; 80/95% ranges by parametric + year-block bootstrap (1,000 draws) Verdict: baseline B = {H[c,p±1,y']: y' in baseline years, y' ≠ y} P_hi = Pr(H > q90(B)) from draws → ≥0.9 "Unusually high" · 0.6–0.9 "Possibly high" · else "Typical" (symmetric for low, shown only where q10(B) is above a minimum activity level) f < 0.30 for all sensors, or range wider than threshold → "Not enough clear views"

Advanced method (stretch, gated): a sensor-response model

Use near-simultaneous Aqua and S-NPP afternoon overpasses (|Δt| ≤ 10 min, before Aqua's drift grows). Merge 375 m VIIRS pixels into roughly 1 km footprint clusters with a grid hash and union-find. Label each cluster as detected or missed by MODIS, counting only where MYD14A1 shows the cell observed. Then fit P(MODIS detects | cluster FRP, pixel count, view angle, land cover, day/night).

The Aqua-equivalent count is Σp, with Poisson-binomial variance Σp(1−p) plus parameter uncertainty. This handles resolution and sensitivity physically rather than through ratios. It mirrors the matching used by Fu et al. 2020 and Zheng et al. 2026.

ML policy: start with logistic regression with splines; try gradient boosting only if held-out Brier score and log-loss improve and the reliability diagram stays calibrated. Gate: only attempt this if the simple method is validated by 23:00 on Friday.

How the algorithm helps the user

Fewer false "unusual" verdicts when satellites change or clouds persist. Analysts can say how sure they are, and see when the tool cannot judge. The overlap bridges also mean the 2027+ NOAA-20/21 era stays comparable to the 25-year record.

Splits and leakage safeguards

  • Temporal: fit the S-NPP → Aqua bridge on 2012–2018 and the NOAA-20 → S-NPP bridge on 2020–2022. Test on 2023–2026. No tuning on test years.
  • Spatial: leave one region out. Fit strata on two regions and test transfer to the third.
  • Baselines: computed leave-one-year-out, so a test year never enters its own baseline.
  • Static mask: build from training years only. Note that FIRMS's mask uses 2023 data.
  • Reproducibility: pipeline runs from raw files with pinned versions. Validation numbers are written to validation.json by code and never typed by hand.

Signature interaction: "Retire the satellites"

What the user does: clicks Simulate 2027 fleet in the "Satellites watching" header, or toggles any satellite.

What changes: Terra, Aqua and S-NPP columns go hollow. The client recombines each period from the remaining satellites' per-sensor estimates, which ship in the payload, in under 200 ms.

  • In Raw mode, 2020–2026 rows recolour: counts collapse and many periods flip class against the MODIS-era baseline.
  • In Adjusted mode, most cells keep their class but fade where ranges widen. Changed cells get a dot.
  • A counter appears: "Periods keeping their verdict, 2023–2026, this area: Raw x% · Adjusted y%." It is computed live and the held-out years are marked.

What the user understands afterwards: the verdicts don't depend on MODIS staying alive. Where they would, Afterglow says so by widening the range.

Honesty rule: the stress test only runs on years held out from bridge fitting (2023–2026). Earlier years are greyed with "used for calibration".

Interaction details

  • Area selection: search across a local gazetteer (geoBoundaries units for the demo countries; no external geocoder). Clicking a unit on the map selects it. Drawing a custom rectangle is a stretch feature with approximate ranges, labelled as such.
  • Time: clicking a year label sets the season profile to that year. Clicking a cell opens the inspector. Shift-click across cells sums a range, shown as one combined verdict with a combined range.
  • Linked views: selecting a period highlights that column across all years and shades the mini-map cells. The profile marks the period.
  • Sensor comparison: the Raw/Adjusted switch; the per-satellite table in the inspector; the stress-test toggles.
  • Evidence: a Worldview link with date, bounding box and fire and true-colour layers; a CSV of detections and per-sensor rows; the method card.
  • Reset and navigation: Esc closes the drawer; Reset restores the full fleet and defaults. State lives in bare hash tokens such as #bd-rangamati-2025-p09, which drive the story presets.

Scientific transparency at the point of use

  • Source observations (raw, per satellite) and derived values (adjusted) always appear in separate labelled columns. Raw is never relabelled.
  • Every verdict states the baseline years, the unit, the range, the coverage and which satellites contributed.
  • Low coverage says "No clear view", never "No fire". Provisional NRT periods carry a dotted outline and the word "provisional".
  • The Method page reads validation.json, which the pipeline wrote. Limitations are listed beside the results, not in a footnote.

Where competitive-programming skills pay off

  • Duplicate and cluster merging: a grid hash plus union-find over 375 m pixels, O(n α(n)).
  • Near-simultaneous MODIS–VIIRS matching: sort by time, a two-pointer window, then spatial hash buckets. O(n log n) instead of all pairs.
  • Static mask: a 400 m hash-count of detections per year, then thresholding. O(n), with streaming over the global monthly files.
  • Area aggregation: precomputed cell-area overlap weights, and prefix sums over time for range sums on shift-select.
  • Bootstrap: vectorized draws (1,000 × periods) in numpy. Year-block resampling uses pre-indexed arrays.

Reproducibility, provenance and fallbacks

  • One command (make all or just all) runs raw files → outputs. The environment is pinned with uv or a conda lock. Data downloads are recorded in provenance.json.

  • Demo fallbacks, in order: live site → offline local build → screen recording → slides with real screenshots.
  • Global usefulness: the pipeline is region-agnostic (any bounding box and tile set). Expansion path: batch-precompute 0.25° monthly globally with cloud compute near the data, then serve per-country JSON.
